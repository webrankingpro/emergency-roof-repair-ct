Connecticut weather and roof observations

Across Connecticut, high winds and nor'easters can drive rain against roof edges and transitions. Snow can add weight to roof structures, while ice dams and repeated freeze-thaw cycles can change how water moves near the eaves. Those broad conditions may matter when a contractor asks about timing, but they do not identify a defect at a particular property.

Questions about Wethersfield requests

Can someone inspect a roof just because I enter a Wethersfield ZIP code?

A ZIP code helps describe the location. It does not confirm that a contractor has accepted the request or that an inspection is arranged. You may be connected with a participating contractor who serves the area.

Should I report a small stain if it is not dripping?

Yes. Explain whether it is new, whether it grows during rain and whether nearby surfaces are damp. A stain alone does not establish an active opening, but the details can help a contractor decide what to ask next.

What if wind damage and a leak appear together?

Describe both observations. Share when the roof change was seen and when water first appeared indoors. Do not climb up to connect the two; a contractor needs to inspect the roof and determine whether they share a source.
