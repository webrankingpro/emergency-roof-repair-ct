First, keep the people and rooms safe

Move small valuables, electronics and furniture away from water if you can reach them safely. Put a stable container under a drip when the ceiling is not sagging. Do not stand below a bulging ceiling or try to puncture it. Water near lights, outlets, switches or visible wiring is an electrical hazard. Keep clear and contact an appropriate emergency service or qualified electrician when needed.

Do not climb onto a wet, icy or wind-affected roof. A ladder can slip, and roof materials may be loose after a storm. Do not pull a branch from the roof or try to push a shingle back into place. Take photographs from a safe place and save the original images. A wide photo of the room or roof side gives context; a closer image records the visible damage.

Choose the emergency roof repair route

Different roof problems need different questions. An active leak may come from a roof opening that is far from the place where water appears indoors. Wind damage may involve raised shingles, flashing that has shifted or an impact from a fallen limb. A low-slope roof may have drainage or membrane concerns that are not the same as a steep shingle roof.

How it works

When you call or submit a request, you may be connected with a participating roofing contractor. The contractor is a separate business that visits the property, inspects the roof and carries out any repairs, and is responsible for its own inspection, services, pricing, licensing and insurance.

Share the property's Connecticut ZIP code, the type of roof problem and what you can safely observe. A contractor may ask when the issue began, whether water is entering and whether a branch or other object struck the roof. A call is not a confirmation that a contractor has accepted the request. The contractor will explain whether it can take the job and discuss any next steps directly with you.

Connecticut weather can expose different roof problems

Nor'easters and high winds can push rain against roof edges and joints. Heavy snow adds load to roof structures. Ice dams can affect drainage near the eaves, and freeze-thaw cycles can stress roof details as water freezes and melts. Older housing stock across much of New England may also have a range of roof designs and materials. These are broad regional conditions, not a diagnosis of any individual roof.

A contractor needs to inspect the property to identify the source of a leak or the extent of storm damage. Do not assume the first visible mark shows the full problem. Keep notes about when water appeared, where the stain is growing and what the weather was doing at the time.

Questions about emergency roof repair

What should I say first when I call?

Start with the property ZIP code and the main issue: active water inside, visible wind damage, a low-slope roof concern or a damaged area that may need temporary protection. Then explain where the problem appears and whether there is an immediate hazard.

Should I wait until the rain stops before asking for help?

You do not need to climb onto the roof or inspect outside during bad weather. You can describe the indoor signs and call with the information you have. Keep away from electrical hazards and unstable ceilings, and use safe photographs when conditions allow.

Does this website perform roof repairs?

No. The work is carried out by independent roofing contractors. This website may connect a caller with a participating roofing contractor, who is solely responsible for its own inspection, services, pricing, licensing and insurance.
