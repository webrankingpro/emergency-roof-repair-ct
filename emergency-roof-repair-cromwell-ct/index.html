Two common reasons to request roof help

An active leak may show as dripping, ceiling discoloration or moisture along an upper wall. Water may travel through the roof structure before reaching the room, so the mark may not be directly below the opening. The leak page explains what to record indoors, how to contain water safely and why an attic search should only happen from stable, dry access.

Storm damage can appear as lifted shingles, loose ridge material, flashing that has shifted or an impact from a branch. A dry room does not prove the roof covering is unchanged. The storm page explains how to photograph visible damage from the ground and when to keep clear of overhead hazards.

Keep the first response simple

Outside, stay off a wet or icy roof. Do not pull loose shingles back into place, apply sealant or move a limb resting on the building. Take photographs from safe ground and make a note of when you noticed the change.

Connecticut weather context

Nor’easters can combine gusts with wind-driven rain, while heavy snow and later thawing can change the load and moisture around roof edges. Ice dams may affect drainage near eaves. These are Connecticut-wide conditions, not a claim about a particular Cromwell property.

Questions about Cromwell service pages

Are the listed ZIP codes confirmed as covered?

No. County and ZIP details are for general location reference only. Give the property's actual ZIP code when you call so coverage can be checked accurately.

Can the storm page help if a branch hit the roof but no shingle fell?

Yes. The page covers impact and wind-related roof concerns as well as missing material. Do not remove the branch or climb to inspect the surface. Photograph the area from a safe position and explain what happened.

Who decides what repair is needed?

The participating roofing contractor that takes the request. The contractor, not this website, inspects roofs, sets prices and performs repairs. Ask the contractor to explain its findings and proposed work before agreeing to a service.
