Describe the issue before selecting a route

Water visible indoors belongs in the leak repair route. That page covers ceiling drips, stains, damp walls and how water can travel from a roof opening before it appears inside. Wind-related changes belong in the storm and wind route. It covers lifted shingles, flashing movement, branches and the difference between an immediate hazard and exterior damage that still needs inspection.

Take safe steps while arranging help

Move light belongings out of a wet room when there is a clear path. Catch a drip with a stable container only if the ceiling is not sagging. Keep away from water near outlets, lights and wiring. If the ceiling is bowed or falling, leave the room. Outdoors, stay clear of branches, roofing pieces and any object that may fall.

Use photographs to record what you can see from a safe location. Include a wide room or roof view and one closer image of the visible change. Do not climb a ladder or step onto a wet, icy or windy roof. A better picture is never worth an unsafe position.

Connecticut-wide roof conditions

Nor’easters and high winds can push rain against roof transitions. Heavy snow adds load, and freeze-thaw cycles can change how water moves around eaves and flashing. These conditions are common across Connecticut; they are not evidence of a defect at one Newington home.

Questions from Newington homeowners

Does a town listing mean a contractor has accepted my request?

No. The listing shows which town pages are available. Service depends on coverage and whether a participating contractor can take the request. The contractor will explain its own service and any proposed work.

What if the roof damage is above a room that looks dry?

Record the visible exterior change and keep an eye on the room from a safe position. Do not wait for a leak to appear before describing a displaced roof component. A contractor can assess whether the roof needs attention.

Can I use the town page to get a price?

The page does not quote roof work. The participating contractor is responsible for discussing its own scope and pricing after learning about the property and, where appropriate, inspecting it.
