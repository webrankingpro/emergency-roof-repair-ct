Pick a route based on the evidence you have

A roof leak page is the right starting point when water appears indoors. It covers ceiling stains, drips, damp wall areas and the way water can travel through a structure before it becomes visible. The storm and wind page is for missing or lifted shingles, shifted flashing, fallen limbs and other changes noticed after strong weather.

Safe observations before you call

For an indoor leak, move small valuables if the area is safe and catch a steady drip with a container on stable flooring. Keep people away from a ceiling that is bowed or cracking. Do not touch a wet light or switch. For exterior damage, do not stand below loose shingles or a damaged tree, and do not climb to photograph an edge.

Connecticut-wide weather can be part of the timeline

A Connecticut storm timeline may include wind-driven rain, heavy snow, ice near roof edges and repeated freeze-thaw cycles. Note which conditions were present when the issue appeared, but do not assume they caused the damage. A roof inspection is needed to identify the source.

Questions about Wallingford roof repair

Why does the town page not list a contractor's name?

The site is designed to route requests to participating contractors rather than represent one roofing company. Contractor availability can vary. The contractor connected with a caller is responsible for describing its business and services.

Can the leak page help when water appears on a wall instead of the ceiling?

Yes. Water may travel inside the roof or wall assembly before becoming visible. Describe the damp area, the floor level and how it changes after rain. Avoid opening wall or ceiling surfaces to search for the source.

Is there a separate Wallingford page for flat roofs?

Not separately. Flat roof repair is covered on the state-wide flat roof page, which applies to Wallingford and the rest of Connecticut.
